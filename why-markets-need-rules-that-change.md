# Why markets need rules that change

A token launch is a bet made in one transaction. The launcher chooses a fee, a split, a liquidity position, and that is the market for the rest of its life.

That is the wrong shape for the thing being launched. Markets have lifecycles:

- At launch nobody is verified, nobody has liquidity, and the right thing to do is protect the pool and the people who show up early.
- Once a creator proves who they are, they should be paid directly, not through an escrow or a promise.
- Once a market is deep, the fee that made sense at ten ETH of liquidity is a tax at a thousand.
- Once a community has hit something real, a product shipped, a number reached, a vote passed, the economics should be allowed to reward it.

Today these moments are handled off-chain, by multisig votes, by manual contract calls, or not at all. Every one of them is a place where trust is required and where things go wrong.

Dynamic Hooks moves these moments into the hook. The rule is written at launch. The *change* happens later, automatically, when the condition is met and proven. The launcher does not hold a key to it. Neither do we.

**Programmable tokens put rules in the asset. Programmable markets put rules in the venue.** The venue is where fees are taken, where liquidity lives, and where every trade already passes through code. That is where the rules belong.
